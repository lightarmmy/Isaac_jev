Isaac combat policy, basic tears and ordinary single-player movement.
Coordinates are game world units; x increases rightward and y downward.
Movement and shooting can happen at the same time. Movement has eight directions
and stay; basic shooting has four cardinal directions and none. A command only
lasts for a few simulation updates. Avoid harmful enemies, projectiles, fire,
spikes, pits when unable to fly, and blocked tiles. Enemy velocity and the recent
state matter: the closest object need not be the most dangerous one. Trajectory
risk estimates assume short constant-velocity motion and are approximate.
Maintain useful firing alignment while keeping an escape route. Invulnerable
or friendly entities are not ordinary attack targets. Active item use is an
independent one-shot decision; its identity, charge, and effect must be supplied.
This first protocol does not cover charge weapons, dual characters, or every
item interaction. Unsupported mechanics are recorded and require added rules
and demonstrations before deploying a policy for them. Never use future draws,
undiscovered map contents, or generator seeds as policy inputs.
Projectiles list the nearest eight hostile bullets relative to the player;
other bullets still contribute to movement risk. Positions, velocities, height,
falling speed and flags describe observed motion, not guaranteed future paths.
History lists past snapshots only; missing entries mean unavailable observations.
The outcome condition is a requested behavior, not knowledge of the future:
positive means observed avoidance of damage under threat, negative means
observed damage-associated behavior, unknown means unassessed behavior.
When positive is requested, favor avoiding damage while maintaining useful fire.
